## Step 1: Stop rules

Competence: Broadcom designs semiconductor products and infrastructure software. Its chips serve AI computing, networking, wireless, broadband and storage markets. Its software includes VMware private cloud, mainframe and security products. It develops technology internally and expands through acquisitions. It reaches customers directly and through distributors, with significant customer concentration. The latest annual survival checks pass: debt/EBITDA is 1.91 versus a maximum of 3.0, and operating profit covers interest 7.94 times versus a minimum of 5. These are fiscal 2025 ratios, not trailing ratios through the latest quarter. Debt due over three years is $12.8B, compared with fiscal 2025 cash of $16.2B and free cash flow of $26.9B. The interim cash balance is $24.0B. Earlier interest-coverage failures show that acquisitions can strain financing; historical debt/EBITDA for fiscal 2020 through 2022 is unavailable because amortization is missing. Operating cash flow exceeds net income in the latest year, but receivables rose from $4.4B to $7.15B during fiscal 2025 and reached $13.7B at the interim date. One customer accounts for 44% of annual-end receivables. This warrants review of collections and payment terms. The 2025 income-tax benefit reflects settlements, expired limitation periods and stock-award tax benefits. No stop rule is identified as failing on the available latest annual evidence; this does not establish that all accounting concerns are resolved.

Decision drivers:

- Debt is 1.91 times EBITDA and interest coverage is 7.94 in fiscal 2025, both inside the policy limits. What would change this: debt-ebitda above 3.0 or interest-coverage below 5, for example after a new debt-financed acquisition, as in fiscal 2019, 2020 and 2024.
- Receivables grow faster than revenue, and one customer holds 44 percent of receivables. What would change this: If receivables continue to grow much faster than revenue, or if the large customer pays late, the trust test can fail.

Open research questions:

- How do customer payment terms and the non-recourse receivables factoring disclosed in the latest 10-Q affect cash conversion and collection risk?
- What evidence supports a sustainable cash-tax rate instead of the illustrative normalization estimate?

## Step 2: Business quality

Business quality score: 3 of 5, an analyst judgment under the policy. ROIC exceeds the 9% weighted average cost of capital (WACC) in 6 of 9 measurable years; the policy requires 7. The first annual ROIC is unavailable because starting invested capital is missing. Fiscal 2025 policy ROIC is 19.9%. At the explicit illustrative 21% normalized tax estimate, annual ROIC is 15.72% and TTM ROIC is 25.83%. These normalized estimates are separate from the historical policy test. Gross margin is 67.8%, operating margin 39.9%, and free cash flow is 42.1% of revenue. These support a strong operating franchise. Incremental ROIC, the added operating return on added capital, is 29.4% over five years, also sensitive to tax effects. One distributor accounts for 32% of revenue; this is not the same as one ultimate customer. The top five end customers account for approximately 40%. The filings identify cyclical semiconductor demand, dependence on AI investment and customers developing their own alternatives. The competitive advantage rests on specialized chip design, networking technology and established software deployments. Acquisitions and customer bargaining power reduce confidence in extrapolating past growth.

Decision drivers:

- ROIC was above the WACC in only 6 of 9 years because of large acquisitions; the policy needs 7. What would change this: At least 7 measurable years above the policy WACC within the rolling lookback, supported by recurring earnings and reviewed tax inputs.
- Revenue depends on a small number of customers: one distributor is 32 percent and the top five are about 40 percent. What would change this: A broader customer base, or long contracts with the large AI customers, would decrease this risk.
- Margins and free cash flow are high and increased over 10 years. What would change this: Sustained deterioration in gross margin and cash conversion would weaken the business-quality score.

Open research questions:

- What sustainable tax rate is supported by the geographic profit mix and tax incentives? The 21% normalization is an estimate, not a verified recurring company rate.
- How much of reported physical depreciation approximates maintenance spending? Acquisition amortization is not automatically treated as maintenance capital.

## Step 3: Management

Management score: 3 of 5, an analyst judgment. Acquisitions have built a profitable semiconductor and software portfolio, but shareholder costs remain material. Diluted shares increased 26.7% across the extracted history, including 11.8% in fiscal 2024 and 1.6% in fiscal 2025. The stock-split adjustment prevents the July 2024 split from appearing as dilution; the VMware acquisition involved shares as well as cash. Stock-based pay was $7.57B, or 11.8% of fiscal 2025 revenue, while reported repurchases were $2.45B. The share-count increase is the direct dilution evidence; comparing repurchase dollars with compensation expense alone cannot measure dilution. Dividends were $11.1B. Goodwill reached $97.8B after the acquisition program, increasing exposure to disappointing acquisition returns. The proxy says 96% of annualized CEO target compensation is variable, mostly tied to long-term stock-price performance. This creates shareholder alignment but does not directly establish a return-on-capital hurdle. Full award terms remain an open review item.

Decision drivers:

- Stock-based pay is 11.8% of revenue, and the diluted share count increased despite repurchases. What would change this: sbc-to-revenue below 0.06 (the level before VMware), or repurchases that decrease the diluted share count for several years.
- Reported incremental ROIC supports acquisition performance, but unusual tax inputs limit that evidence. What would change this: A new large acquisition that decreases ROIC below 0.09 for more than two years.

Open research questions:

- Read the full proxy statement: which performance hurdles apply to the September 2025 CEO PSU award?

## Step 4: Value

The starting FCFF is the three-year average of $18.6B, and the calculator treats stock-based compensation as a cash-flow cost. Per-share values use the August 2026 share count and interim cash and debt. Missing preferred stock and minority interest are assumed_zero, which affects claims deducted from business value; missing historical intangibles are also assumed_zero in some asset calculations. The resulting DCF estimates are $63.51 bear, $100.83 base and $136.18 bull. Reverse DCF solves for the growth needed to justify the supplied price: 26.5% annually for 10 years under the base discount and terminal assumptions. Historical revenue growth was 21.7% over five years and includes acquisitions; it is not directly equivalent to organic FCFF growth. Earnings value at the policy multiple is $38.55 per share. Tangible book value and net current asset value are negative and do not provide a positive asset-based valuation floor. Revenue reverse DCF is unavailable without an explicit target margin. A sum-of-the-parts analysis could value software and semiconductors separately, but must allocate unallocated expenses and stock-based pay; no numerical SOTP estimate is included. The mature growth cap and averaging of historical cash flow can understate value if durable AI growth proves materially stronger; the current conclusion is conditional on the policy. The refreshed comparison adds TTM revenue of $89.10B, TTM FCF of $39.40B, TTM FCFF of $33.87B, and TTM P/E of 45.10 times. From TTM FCFF, the conditional reverse DCF requires 18.3% annual growth, versus 26.5% from the historical average; neither is a direct observation of market expectations. Their margins, tax, capital intensity and growth paths are explicit estimates rather than management guidance. The comparison contains yearly operating forecasts and sensitivity results; its values do not change the conservative policy screen.

## Step 5: Decision

The overall quality score is 3, the lower of the business and management scores. With high evidence from 10 annual periods, the policy requires a 40% margin of safety. Margin of safety means (estimated value minus price) divided by estimated value; the base result is −258.6% and the bear result is −469.2%. A negative margin means price exceeds model value; it is not a predicted investment loss. The result is does_not_meet_rules. Latest annual revenue and owner earnings increased, so this review does not identify a persistent operating decline; this alone cannot rule out a future value trap. The supplied price is above the three policy DCF estimates and the separate illustrative forward cases. This is a conditional model result, not a universal claim about intrinsic value. AI demand and software profitability provide possible business reasons for investor optimism, but filings do not establish the market motivations behind the price. No market-news or sentiment analysis is included.

Decision drivers:

- The base margin of safety is −2.59 against a requirement of 0.40. What would change this: margin-of-safety-base reaches 0.40 or more, from a much lower price or from a much higher base DCF value.
- The quality score is 3 because of the ROIC rule, customer concentration and dilution. What would change this: With a score of 4, the requirement falls to 0.25, but the result does not change at this price.

## Step 6: Commitment

Draft monitoring conditions use policy thresholds and explicitly identified analyst review triggers. 1) Debt/EBITDA above the policy maximum of 3.0. 2) Interest coverage below the policy minimum of 5. 3) Latest annual ROIC at or below the 9% WACC. 4) Stock-based pay/revenue above the fiscal 2025 baseline of 11.8%, an analyst review trigger rather than a policy stop. 5) The top distributor share above the reported 32% baseline, also an analyst review trigger. The ROIC history rule is already unmet; it requires improvement rather than future invalidation. Pre-mortem, a hypothetical scenario rather than a prediction: AI customers reduce capital spending or develop competing chips, software customers resist pricing changes, receivable collection slows, and cash growth fails to match the valuation assumptions. Reassess using the same policy and reviewed filing data rather than assuming that a lower market price alone improves business quality.

Decision drivers:

- The main risk is the valuation, together with high customer concentration. What would change this: A price that gives a base margin of safety of 0.40 or more.

Open research questions:

- What evidence would justify revising the explicit segment growth and margin estimates?
- What sustainable cash-tax rate and maintenance-capital estimate should replace the illustrative assumptions?
