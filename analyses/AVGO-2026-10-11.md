Broadcom designs semiconductor products and infrastructure software, so the operating-company FCFF and ROIC model is suitable. Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF) is cash available to all capital providers; return on invested capital (ROIC) measures operating profit after tax relative to the capital used. Annual history covers fiscal 2016 through fiscal 2025, with the latest annual period ending 2025-11-02.

The newest extracted balance sheet is dated 2026-08-02; the latest filing date is 2026-09-10. Fiscal 2018 and fiscal 2024 each cover 53 weeks. Historical share counts are adjusted for stock splits. Restated values and conflicting accounting tags require review; these markers do not by themselves establish an accounting problem.

The 2025 tax benefit causes the calculator to use a zero tax rate; the 2024 rate reaches its upper limit. These rates are not normalized estimates. D&A for 2020–2022 contains only depreciation in its source parts, so historical EBITDA and owner earnings for those years are incomplete.

The latest cover-page share count is dated 2026-08-28 and does not require a manually sourced share count.

Competence: Broadcom designs semiconductor products and infrastructure software. Its chips serve AI computing, networking, wireless, broadband and storage markets. Its software includes VMware private cloud, mainframe and security products.

It develops technology internally and expands through acquisitions. It reaches customers directly and through distributors, with significant customer concentration. The latest annual survival checks pass: debt/EBITDA is 1.91 versus a maximum of 3.0, and operating profit covers interest 7.94 times versus a minimum of 5.

These are fiscal 2025 ratios, not trailing ratios through the latest quarter. Debt due over three years is $12.8B, compared with fiscal 2025 cash of $16.2B and free cash flow of $26.9B. The interim cash balance is $24.0B.

Earlier interest coverage failures show that acquisitions can strain financing; historical debt/EBITDA for 2020–2022 also suffers from missing amortization. Operating cash flow exceeds net income in the latest year, but receivables rose from $4.4B to $7.15B during fiscal 2025 and reached $13.7B at the interim date. One customer accounts for 44% of annual-end receivables.

This warrants review of collections and payment terms. The 2025 income-tax benefit reflects settlements, expired limitation periods and stock-award tax benefits. No stop rule is identified as failing on the available latest annual evidence; this does not establish that all accounting concerns are resolved.

**Decision drivers and what could change them**

- Debt is 1.91 times EBITDA and interest coverage is 7.94 in fiscal 2025, both inside the policy limits. **What changes the assessment:** debt/EBITDA above 3.0 or interest coverage below 5, for example after a new debt-financed acquisition, as in fiscal 2019, 2020 and 2024.
- Receivables grow faster than revenue, and one customer holds 44 percent of receivables. **What changes the assessment:** If receivables continue to grow much faster than revenue, or if the large customer pays late, the trust test can fail.

**Open questions**

- What payment terms and collection risks explain the interim increase in receivables? The extraction does not include the latest 10-Q narrative for this company.
- What recurring tax rate should replace the unusual reported tax effects in a separate sensitivity analysis?

Business quality score: 3 of 5, an analyst judgment under the policy. ROIC exceeds the 9% weighted average cost of capital (WACC) in 6 of 9 measurable years; the policy requires 7. The first annual ROIC is unavailable because starting invested capital is missing.

Fiscal 2025 ROIC is 19.9%, but its zero tax-rate input makes it sensitive to tax normalization; no normalized case has been calculated. Gross margin is 67.8%, operating margin 39.9%, and free cash flow is 42.1% of revenue. These support a strong operating franchise.

Incremental ROIC, the added operating return on added capital, is 29.4% over five years, also sensitive to tax effects. One distributor accounts for 32% of revenue; this is not the same as one ultimate customer. The top five end customers account for approximately 40%.

The filings identify cyclical semiconductor demand, dependence on AI investment and customers developing their own alternatives. The competitive advantage rests on specialized chip design, networking technology and established software deployments. Acquisitions and customer bargaining power reduce confidence in extrapolating past growth.

**Decision drivers and what could change them**

- ROIC was above the WACC in only 6 of 9 years because of large acquisitions; the policy needs 7. **What changes the assessment:** At least 7 measurable years above the policy WACC within the rolling lookback, supported by recurring earnings and reviewed tax inputs.
- Revenue depends on a small number of customers: one distributor is 32 percent and the top five are about 40 percent. **What changes the assessment:** A broader customer base, or long contracts with the large AI customers, would decrease this risk.
- Margins and free cash flow are high and increased over 10 years. **What changes the assessment:** Sustained deterioration in gross margin and cash conversion would weaken the business-quality score.

**Open questions**

- Do you want to add back the amortization of acquired intangibles to examine ROIC on the business without the effect of acquisitions? This is a less conservative view.
- Which tax rate do you think is normal for Broadcom after the Singapore incentives end in 2030?

Management score: 3 of 5, an analyst judgment. Acquisitions have built a profitable semiconductor and software portfolio, but shareholder costs remain material. Diluted shares increased 26.7% across the extracted history, including 11.8% in fiscal 2024 and 1.6% in fiscal 2025.

The stock-split adjustment prevents the July 2024 split from appearing as dilution; the VMware acquisition involved shares as well as cash. Stock-based pay was $7.57B, or 11.8% of fiscal 2025 revenue, while reported repurchases were $2.45B. Dividends were $11.1B. Goodwill reached $97.8B after the acquisition program, increasing exposure to disappointing acquisition returns. The proxy says 96% of annualized CEO target compensation is variable, mostly tied to long-term stock-price performance.

This creates shareholder alignment but does not directly establish a return-on-capital hurdle. Full award terms remain an open review item.

**Decision drivers and what could change them**

- Stock-based pay is 11.8% of revenue, and the diluted share count increased despite repurchases. **What changes the assessment:** stock-based compensation as a share of revenue below 0.06 (the level before VMware), or repurchases that decrease the diluted share count for several years.
- Reported incremental ROIC supports acquisition performance, but unusual tax inputs limit that evidence. **What changes the assessment:** A new large acquisition that decreases ROIC below 0.09 for more than two years.

**Open questions**

- Read the full proxy statement: which performance hurdles apply to the September 2025 CEO PSU award?

Discounted cash flow (DCF) estimates the present value of future cash available to capital providers. The policy estimates use WACC of 9%, a 10-year forecast and a base growth cap of 10%. Scenario growth estimates are 5%, 10% and 13%; terminal growth estimates are 2%, 2.5% and 3%.

These are policy assumptions, not forecasts supplied by management. The starting FCFF is the three-year average of $18.6B, and the calculator treats stock-based compensation as a cash-flow cost. Per-share values use the August 2026 share count and interim cash and debt.

Missing preferred stock and minority interest are assumed to be zero, which affects claims deducted from business value; missing historical intangibles are also assumed_zero in some asset calculations. The resulting DCF estimates are $63.51 bear, $100.83 base and $136.18 bull. Reverse DCF solves for the growth needed to justify the supplied price: 26.5% annually for 10 years under the base discount and terminal assumptions.

Historical revenue growth was 21.7% over five years and includes acquisitions; it is not directly equivalent to organic FCFF growth. Earnings value at the policy multiple is $38.55 per share. Tangible book value and net current asset value are negative and do not provide a positive asset-based valuation floor.

Revenue reverse DCF is unavailable without an explicit target margin. A sum-of-the-parts analysis could value software and semiconductors separately, but must allocate unallocated expenses and stock-based pay; no numerical SOTP estimate is included. The mature growth cap and averaging of historical cash flow can understate value if durable AI growth proves materially stronger; the current conclusion is conditional on the policy.

**Decision drivers and what could change them**

- The supplied price requires 26.5% annual FCFF growth for 10 years under the model. **What changes the assessment:** A lower price or a sourced growth scenario that produces the required margin of safety would change the valuation test.
- Even the bull DCF value ($136.18) is far below the price. **What changes the assessment:** Explicitly revised growth or discount-rate estimates would need to raise model value enough to meet the required margin of safety.

**Open questions**

- Do you accept the growth limit of 0.10 in the policy for a company whose AI revenue grows quickly? A higher limit is less conservative.

## Step 5: Decision under your policy

The overall quality score is 3, the lower of the business and management scores. With high evidence from 10 annual periods, the policy requires a 40% margin of safety. Margin of safety means (estimated value minus price) divided by estimated value; the base result is −258.6% and the bear result is −469.2%.

A negative margin means price exceeds model value; it is not a predicted investment loss. The result is does_not_meet_rules. Latest annual revenue and owner earnings increased, so this review does not identify a persistent operating decline; this alone cannot rule out a future value trap.

The supplied price is above all three DCF estimates. AI demand and software profitability provide possible business reasons for investor optimism, but filings do not establish the market motivations behind the price. No market-news or sentiment analysis is included.

**Decision drivers and what could change them**

- The base margin of safety is −2.59 against a requirement of 0.40. **What changes the assessment:** base margin of safety reaches 0.40 or more, from a much lower price or from a much higher base DCF value.
- The quality score is 3 because of the ROIC rule, customer concentration and dilution. **What changes the assessment:** With a score of 4, the requirement falls to 0.25, but the result does not change at this price.

## Terms used in this report

- **DCF:** Discounted cash flow. An estimate of future cash flows expressed in today's money.
- **FCFF:** Free cash flow to the firm. Cash available to debt and equity capital providers.
- **ROIC:** Return on invested capital. Operating profit after tax relative to capital used.
- **WACC:** Weighted average cost of capital. The model's discount rate.
- **EBITDA:** Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization.
- **D&A:** Depreciation and amortization. Accounting charges for the use of assets.
- **NCAV:** Net current asset value. Current assets minus total liabilities.
